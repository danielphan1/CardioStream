| Constant | Value | Why this number |

## Typography

Unchanged from `index.css` — **4 sizes, 2 weights, zero additions**. The Two-Weight Rule forbids a third weight (Atkinson ships only 400 and 700 static files; 600 would be synthesized).

| Role | Token | Size | Weight | Line Height | Used in this phase for |
|------|-------|------|--------|-------------|------------------------|
| Body | `text-base` | 18px (1.125rem) | 400 | 1.5 | the live filter sentence, the vitals min–max line, helper copy |
| Label | `text-label` | 20px (1.25rem) | 700 | 1.25 | every rail item, every trigger, every chip, every button, the vitals label, **the product `<h1>`** |
| Heading | `text-heading` | 24px (1.5rem) | 700 | 1.2 | the popover heading ("Filters"), the nav panel heading, the Readings page heading |
| Display | `text-display` | 36px (2.25rem) | 700 | 1.15 | the four vitals values |

The 18px Floor Rule holds everywhere. **No text in the new shell renders below 18px.** The reference dashboards' 11–13px labels are explicitly rejected (CONTEXT `<constraints>`).

**The product title is one `<h1>`, one string, one token, at every breakpoint** (R1/title correction): the element is `<h1>`, the string is **"Chris's Health Dashboard"** (today's `Header.tsx:170` text — "CardioStream" is CONTEXT's internal nickname for the project and is **not** a user-facing string anywhere in the codebase; it is not introduced here), and the token is `text-label` (20px/700) in **both** the rail and the slim bar. Size is held constant across breakpoints deliberately: a title that changes size with the viewport makes the document outline viewport-dependent. `text-label` rather than today's `text-heading` because at 24px/700 the string wraps to three lines in a 208px rail item and pushes the phone's top bar past 100px, which contradicts this phase's density goal; 20px/700 is still the second-largest token in the system and clears the 18px floor. Exactly one `<h1>` is in the DOM at any viewport — see §5.2's one-shell-mounted rule.

### 4.3 Resolution of the three must-resolve items

#### (1) `--line-diastolic` — derived, not picked

**New values: `#AC40BF` (light) / `#B055BE` (dark).** A deep violet.

*Derivation.* The hue wheel is almost full. Occupied light-theme hues: 3.1 (crisis), 4.4 (hazard), 7.5 (stage 2), 18.7 (pulse), 23.9 (stage 1), 33.3 (brass→focus), 47.5 (elevated), 74.8 (procedures), 156.5 (normal), 168.6 (**the new accent**), 204.0 (hypotension / bradycardia ref), 214.2 (systolic), 216.0 (ink), 263.6 (labs), 325.1 (incidents). That leaves exactly two windows wider than ±20°:

| Window | Centre | Half-gap | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 74.8 → 156.5 | **115.7** (green) | 40.9° | **Rejected.** Green *means "Normal"* in this palette, and the Normal timeline band is drawn behind the lines. A green diastolic line over a green Normal band is the same class of error the client rejected when they refused teal-on-the-Normal-chip. |
| 263.6 → 325.1 | **294.4** (violet) | 30.8° | **Selected.** No clinical meaning attached to violet; 27° from labs, 34° from incidents, 77° from systolic, **122° from the new accent**. |

*Greyscale derivation.* In light theme every plotted line must sit at relative luminance Y ≤ 0.2388 to clear 3:1 against `--color-mist`, which caps how far any line can separate from systolic (Y = 0.0413). Within the violet window the maximum achievable separation at palette-register saturation is 2.3–2.4:1. The chosen values were selected for **no greyscale regression in light and a 2× improvement in dark**:

| Pair (greyscale / luminance ratio) | Today | After | |
|---|---|---|---|
| light systolic ÷ diastolic | 2.22 | **2.32** | improved |
| light diastolic ÷ pulse | 1.17 | **1.23** | improved |
| light systolic ÷ pulse | 1.89 | 1.89 | unchanged — carried by the dashed pulse stroke |
| dark systolic ÷ diastolic | **1.12** | **2.24** | 2× improvement (this was the palette's worst number) |
| dark diastolic ÷ pulse | 1.29 | **1.96** | improved |
| dark systolic ÷ pulse | 1.14 | 1.14 | unchanged — carried by the dashed pulse stroke |

> **Correction to CONTEXT.** CONTEXT states "systolic and diastolic currently sit at only 1.89:1 luminance separation". Measured from `index.css`: light systolic ÷ diastolic is **2.22:1**; the 1.89:1 figure is light **systolic ÷ pulse** (which is what `CombinedTimeline.tsx`'s comment actually says, and what the dashed pulse stroke exists to solve). The genuinely broken pair was **dark** systolic ÷ diastolic at **1.12:1** — effectively identical in greyscale — and this phase fixes it.

Separation from the two violet-adjacent overlay colors:

| Pair | Hue Δ | Lightness Δ | Collision box (10°/10pp) |
|---|---|---|---|
| light diastolic `#AC40BF` vs labs `#6B4A9E` | 27.4° | 4.5pp | clears on hue, 2.7× margin |
| light diastolic vs incidents `#9C2E6E` | 34.1° | 10.4pp | clears on both |
| dark diastolic `#B055BE` vs labs `#C3A0E8` | 22.8° | 22.9pp | clears on both |
| dark diastolic vs incidents `#EFA0C9` | 36.9° | 24.3pp | clears on both |

Overlays also render as `◆ ▲ ■` glyph markers, never as a line, and the Show panel legend draws each series' exact stroke — so the non-colour signal is already in place.

`--line-pulse` stays `#9E4A24` / `#E3A07C`. The **pulse stroke stays dashed** — that is still the only thing separating pulse from systolic in greyscale, and `CombinedTimeline.test.tsx`'s dashed assertion must keep passing.

*Why there is no "line vs accent" assertion.* The new light diastolic `#AC40BF` and the new light accent `#0D826C` are luminance-near-identical (1.04:1). That is harmless — §4.1 bars the accent from every data surface, so the two never appear adjacent, and the real invariant is the **122° hue separation** recorded above. A test asserting the hexes merely differ would pass trivially and guard nothing; a genuine hue-separation assertion would require a hue helper that `contrast.test.ts` does not have and that this phase does not need. Recorded here instead of asserted, deliberately, so a future palette edit sees the margin.

#### (2) The focus ring — brass confirmed, with one gap closed

**`--color-signal: #8A5A1E` (light) / `#D9A356` (dark)** — the client's candidate, confirmed. Rationale:

- Warm (hue 33) against a cool accent (hue 169) — **135° apart**, the largest separation available.
- Both pairs are **already asserted in `contrast.test.ts`** today under the `brass` keys, so no new derivation is needed: light 5.48:1 vs deck / 4.86:1 vs mist; dark 8.33:1 / 7.52:1.
- Retiring `#0F6E86` (hue 192.1) removes a token that would otherwise sit 23° from the accent — exactly the collapse DESIGN.md forbids.

Two constraints the implementation must honour:

- **The 2px `outline-offset` is load-bearing.** Focus ring against an accent fill is 1.24:1 (light) and 1.00:1 (dark) — luminance-identical in dark. The ring is only ever judged against the surface *behind* the control because the 2px offset exposes a band of deck/mist between fill and ring. Do not remove or zero the offset, and do not switch the ring to `box-shadow` (which has no offset semantics here).
- **Gap found and closed: the Command Bar's dark panel.** The Command Bar sits on `--color-panel` (`#101C2E` light / `#050A12` dark — non-inverting, always dark). Against `#101C2E`, the light focus ring is **2.90:1 — below the 3:1 floor**. This is *pre-existing*, not a regression (today's `#0F6E86` scores 2.93:1 there), but this spec closes it rather than inheriting it. Add a **non-inverting** focus token, mirroring the existing `--color-panel-text` precedent:

```css
/* declared identically in :root and .dark — non-inverting, like --color-panel-text */
--color-signal-on-panel: #D9A356;
```
```css
[data-surface="panel"] :focus-visible { outline-color: var(--color-signal-on-panel); }
```

Add `data-surface="panel"` to the Command Bar `<section>` in `App.tsx`. Scores **7.60:1** (light panel) / 8.81:1 (dark panel).

#### (3) The accent's two theme values — SETTLED

**`--color-accent: #0D826C` (light) / `#33C1A6` (dark).** Client-confirmed 2026-09-30. There is no alternative on the table: the `#0E8C74` option CONTEXT originally named is **withdrawn**, not recorded as a fallback, for the reason below.

| | Light | Dark |
|---|---|---|
| `--color-accent` | **`#0D826C`** | **`#33C1A6`** |
| `--color-accent-text` | `#FFFFFF` | `#0A121F` |
| text-on-fill contrast | **4.74:1** | **8.33:1** |
| vs `--color-deck` | 4.41:1 | 8.33:1 |
| vs `--color-mist` | 3.91:1 | 7.51:1 |
| vs `--color-panel` (Send) | 3.61:1 | 8.80:1 |
| hue / sat / lightness | 168.7 / 81.8 / 28.0 | 168.6 / 58 / 48 |
| relative luminance | 0.1713 | **0.4160** |

The dark value was derived for **luminance parity with the brass it replaces** (`#D9A356`, Y = 0.4162 — a match to four decimals), so the dark theme's perceived accent weight does not change, and at the **same hue as the light accent** (168.6) so the accent has one identity across themes.

**Why `#0E8C74` is deleted rather than kept as an option.** Revision 1 argued it was only a gate failure, not a WCAG failure, because every accent-filled text surface in the codebase is 20px/700 (22 sites verified) and therefore WCAG *large* text at a 3:1 floor. That argument no longer holds: §5.3 of this same spec introduces a **23rd** accent-filled text surface, the Filters count badge, at `text-base` — **18px/400**. WCAG 1.4.3's large-text threshold is 24px normal or 18.66px bold; 18px/400 is **neither**. White on `#0E8C74` is 4.18:1, so with the badge in the design `#0E8C74` would be a genuine **WCAG 1.4.3 AA failure**, not merely a failed project gate. `#0D826C` is the same hue (168.7) and saturation (81.8) at lightness 28.0 instead of 30.2 — indistinguishable side by side — and clears the 4.5:1 floor at 4.74:1 with no test relaxation. Every number elsewhere in this file already assumes `#0D826C`.

## 5. Component Contracts

### 5.0 Shell structure, overlay state, and the z-layer stack

Three surfaces in this phase can be open over the content: the **nav panel** (<1024px), the **Filters popover/panel**, and the **Dates popover/panel**. `GuideOverlay` is a fourth, already shipped. They must not fight each other, so the shell declares one state and one layer stack.

**One overlay at a time.** `App.tsx` holds a single local state `openOverlay: "nav" | "filters" | "dates" | null` — mutually exclusive by construction, no booleans to desynchronise. Opening any of the three also calls the existing `useGuide()` close action, so the guide and these three are mutually exclusive too. Nothing is added to `store/filters.ts` (§8) — this is transient UI state, not agent-addressable state.

```ts
const anyOverlayOpen = guideOpen || openOverlay !== null;
```

**One measured top band.** The slim bar (<1024px) and the Command Bar `<section>` are wrapped in a single `<div ref={shellTopRef}>`. That wrapper carries `sticky top-0 z-[60]` while `anyOverlayOpen` — replacing today's `${guideOpen ? " sticky top-0 z-[60]" : ""}` on the Command Bar section alone (`App.tsx:236`), which is the R3 widening. The wrapper is **never `inert`**: the mic and the Command Bar must stay reachable with any overlay open (D-03/D-04), and the menu trigger inside it stays live so a second tap closes the panel it opened. `useClearanceHeight(shellTopRef)` then returns one number for every panel's `top` offset, including `GuideOverlay`'s `clearanceAbove` (one ref instead of today's two; `GuideOverlay` itself is untouched — only the argument it receives changes).

**`useClearanceHeight` must be lifted.** It is a local function at `App.tsx:52`, not an exported hook. Move it verbatim to `frontend/src/hooks/useClearanceHeight.ts` and export it; `App.tsx` imports it, and so do the panels that need their own measurement. Do not duplicate it and do not thread the number through props from a sibling that cannot see it.

**`inert` scope (R2 resolution).** `inert` goes on `<main>`, exactly as today, with a widened condition — **and the two full-viewport panels render as siblings of `<main>`, not inside it**:

| Overlay | Rendered where | `<main>` inert while open? |
|---|---|---|
| `GuideOverlay` | sibling of `<main>` (today, `App.tsx:240`) | yes |
| Nav panel (<1024px) | sibling of `<main>` | yes |
| Filters / Dates panel at **<768px** | **sibling of `<main>`** | yes |
| Filters / Dates popover at **≥768px** | inside `<main>`, immediately after its trigger | **no** |

### 5.4 The D-20 live filter sentence — where it lives and why

**Immediately below the trigger row, in the content column, always rendered, never inside any popover.** 8px below the trigger row, 24px above the vitals strip. **One** `<div aria-live="polite">` holding three stacked `<p>` lines at `text-base` (18px/400), `var(--color-depth)`:

1. The filter sentence — `FilterBar`'s existing builder, verbatim: `"All data · All times of day · All categories · All pulse categories"`.
2. The Show sentence — `buildShowSentence(visibleDatasets)`, verbatim.
3. Conditionally, the existing note `"These datasets show on the Timeline. Switch back to see them."` when `chartView !== "timeline"`.

**This is a hard requirement, not a preference.** It is how the primary user — who cannot reliably open a popover and cannot see one he has not opened — knows what is applied. Burying any of these three lines inside the popover would mean an agent-applied filter change announces to nothing while the popover is closed, which is the normal state. The strings themselves are **unchanged**; this phase relocates the markup.

**Announcement ordering — one region, not three.** Today the three sentences carry three *separate* `aria-live="polite"` attributes in two components (`FilterBar.tsx:271`, `ShowPanel.tsx:108` and `:115`). Co-locating them without consolidating would let one agent apply queue three adjacent polite announcements on top of the Command Bar's own live region **and** the spoken `composeConfirmation` — four or five utterances for one command, in an order no screen reader guarantees. So:

- The **wrapper** `<div>` carries `aria-live="polite"` and default `aria-atomic="false"`. The three `<p>` elements **lose their individual live attributes.** A change to one line announces that line only; a change to several announces them in DOM order, which is the reading order on screen. One region, deterministic sequence.
- The **Command Bar keeps its own single `aria-live` region** and its spoken `composeConfirmation`, unchanged. That region is the *acknowledgement* ("showing your last 30 days"); this block is the *state* ("Last 30 days · Mornings · …"). They are allowed to overlap in content — the spoken confirmation is what the primary user acts on, and this block is the visible and AT-readable record of what is currently applied.
- Nothing in this block is `aria-atomic="true"` and nothing uses `assertive`. A filter change is never urgent enough to interrupt the mic.

## 9. Known risks, measured

### 9.1 `categoryBarRightMargin` — no new narrowest case

CONTEXT flags the rail as "giving `categoryBarRightMargin` a new narrowest case". Measured arithmetic says otherwise:

| Viewport | Gutters | Rail | Chart container |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1440px (today) | 128 | — | 1280 − 128 = **1152** |
| 1440px (after) | 128 | 240 | 1200 − 128 = **1072** |
| 1024px (after, rail present) | 64 | 240 | 1024 − 240 − 64 = **720** |
| 1023px (after, rail is a panel) | 64 | 0 | **959** |
| **375px (today and after)** | 32 | 0 | **343** |

The rail is suppressed below 1024px, so it never reserves width at phone sizes. **The global narrowest container stays 343px and is unchanged by this phase.** `clampCategoryBarRightMargin` + `truncateLabelForWidth` already cover it (they were built for exactly that case in Phase 16's gap closure), and `chartData.test.ts`'s hardcoded 383/295/40 are outputs of `estimateChipWidth` — pure functions of label text and font size, not of container width — so they are **not** invalidated. Re-run the suite; do not pre-emptively retune `CHIP_CHAR_WIDTH_FACTOR`. Its own comment already says to bump it only against a real observed clip, never a guess.

### 9.2 Accent hue vs the Normal category

`--color-accent` at hue 168.6 sits **12.1°** from light `--cat-normal` (156.5) and **18.6°** from dark `--cat-normal` (150.0). Both clear DESIGN.md's 10° collision box, but 12.1° is the tightest margin in the palette. It is acceptable because the two never share an element: category chips are tinted in their own hue with a hue-matched border and a hue-matched dot, and the accent never touches a data surface. Recorded so a future palette edit knows the margin is thin.

### 9.3 Focus ring hue vs Stage 1 / pulse

`--color-signal` at hue 33.3 sits 9.4° from `--cat-stage1` (23.9) and 14.6° from `--line-pulse` (18.7). **This collision already exists in the shipped product** (brass is the accent today at the same hue) and is not worsened by demoting brass to the focus ring — the ring is transient, 3px, offset, and shaped. Not a blocker; recorded.

### 9.4 The vitals secondary has 9.9px of slack at 1024px

See §5.6's warning box. The shortened `98–142` string needs 146.1px of a 156.0px cell at the narrowest rail-present viewport — about 6%. Any future addition to that line (a unit, a wider separator, a four-digit value) breaks the locked ~90px row height, and the only correct response is a fresh in-browser measurement pass. Recorded here so the risk is visible outside the section that created it.
