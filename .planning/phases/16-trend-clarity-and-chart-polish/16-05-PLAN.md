<objective>
Close the one remaining BLOCKER from `16-VERIFICATION.md` round 2 (score 11/13): round 1's `categoryBarRightMargin()` correctly sizes `margin.right` to cover the longest D-10 label, but never bounds that value against the container's actual measured width. Recharts hard-clamps its own plot area (`offsetWidth = Math.max(chartWidth - offset.left - offset.right, 0)`, `frontend/node_modules/recharts/es6/state/selectors/selectChartOffsetInternal.js`) — the moment `margin.left + margin.right >= containerWidth`, every category bar renders with zero width (invisible) and the D-10 label text starts near the container's left edge with far less room than it needs. The verifier reproduced this today with the real 132-reading production dataset (longest label "Hypertensive Crisis — 7 readings (5%)", 383px margin needed) at a 375px viewport (iPhone SE), where `App.tsx`'s `px-4` page padding leaves CategoryBars only ~343px of container width — well under the ~391px the unclamped margin needs.

Purpose: `CategoryBars.tsx`'s bars and its D-10 labels ("the labels ARE the values") must both stay visible on any real phone viewport this app supports — degrading gracefully (a shrunk-but-legible label, visible bars) rather than catastrophically (invisible bars) when the container is too narrow for the full label.

Output: two new pure functions in `frontend/src/lib/chartData.ts` — `clampCategoryBarRightMargin()` (bounds the margin against containerWidth, reserving a minimum plot width) and `truncateLabelForWidth()` (shrinks a label with an ellipsis to fit a pixel budget) — both regression-tested against the real 343px production scenario, and `CategoryBars.tsx` wired to use both so the round-2 gap closes at its root cause rather than in isolation.
</objective>
